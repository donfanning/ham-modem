# G3RUH 19200 / 38400 bd over 48 kHz legs — 2026-10-21

`modems/g3ruh.py` generalised over baud (9600/19200/38400): 5 internal samples per symbol (48/96/192 kHz), roll-off
chosen to keep the band under 96% of the leg's Nyquist (38400 on 48 kHz: beta 0.2), and a rate plan that refuses
legs which cannot hold the band. 48 kHz legs use L16/48000 with 10 ms packets so a packet fits a 1500-byte MTU.

## Offline (40 frames of ~250 bytes)

| modem @ leg | noise 0/1k/2k/3k rms | drift | CPU |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9600 @ 16k | 40/40/40/39 | 39 (+500 ppm) | 1.4% |
| 19200 @ 32k / 48k | 40/40/40/39 | 40 | 2.4% |
| 38400 @ 48k | 40/40/38/32 (after filter tuning) | 40 @ 100 ppm, 39 @ 500 ppm (band-limited) | 4.2% |
| 38400 @ 96k | 40/40/40/40 | 40 | 4.2% |

Lesson: `impair.drift` (linear interpolation) is itself a strong low-pass near Nyquist and made 38400 @ 48k look
broken (32/40); `impair.drift_bandlimited` shows the modem is fine. Tests use the band-limited model at these rates.

## Live, lab PBX, room 7348 (ConfBridge 48 kHz, 10 ms mixing)

- Echo 7399 at L16/48000: 5/5 at 19200 and at 38400, bad FCS 0.
- **Bug found and fixed:** Asterisk sends 20 ms packets even when we offer `ptime:10` (and with
  `allow=slin48:10`). Our receive path measured timestamp gaps against *our* frame size and concealed 10 ms after
  every packet (0/5 decoded). Gaps are now measured against the previous packet's own length (test added).
- 3 stations x 8 frames, 1 s spacing (busy): 42/48 at both speeds; 4 s spacing at 19200: 36/36, bad FCS 0. The
  losses were contention: the collision window is the bridge/packet latency (~30-40 ms), independent of baud.
- Same busy test with `--collision-detect`: 48/48 at 19200 (0 aborts) and 48/48 at 38400 (6 aborts recovered).
  Single runs each.

## Open

- Asterisk's 20 ms L16/48000 packets are 1932-byte UDP datagrams (IP-fragmented). Harmless on the lab bridge;
  on real networks find the framing setting that Asterisk honours, or use 32 kHz legs for 19200.
